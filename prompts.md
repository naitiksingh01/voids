Build a full-stack web app called "Cohort Interviewer" — an AI-powered technical interview agent for graduates of a 31-day AI engineering bootcamp. This is a hackathon submission judged on realism of the interview experience, personalization quality, and polish. Treat this as a real product, not a demo toy.
1. What this app does The app conducts a live, multi-turn, conversational technical interview with a candidate, based on that specific candidate's actual learning history in the cohort — not a generic question bank. It should feel like a sharp senior engineer interviewing them, referencing their real progress: what they struggled with, what they skipped, what they aced on the first try, and what their job role implies they should be strong in.

Role fit: use jobRole to weight which modules matter most (e.g. a "DevOps Engineer" should get more depth on Day 28 Docker/Kubernetes deployment and observability; an "AI Engineer" should get pushed harder on RAG, agents, and MCP; a "Business Analyst" should be interviewed more on conceptual understanding and less on low-level implementation). Show this reasoning transparently somewhere in the UI (e.g. a collapsible "Interviewer's prep notes" panel) — this is a strong hackathon differentiator: judges can see the agent reasoning about the candidate, not just chatting.
   4.   4. Interview flow requirements Minimum 8 questions, spanning at least 4 different curriculum days, pulled from days the candidate actually completed (weighted by the personalization logic above). Every question after the first should be capable of being a follow-up derived from the candidate's previous answer — if they give a shallow answer, drill deeper on the same concept before moving on; if they nail it, move to a harder related concept or pivot module. Maintain full conversation context across turns (this is a multi-turn stateful conversation, not independent Q&A). Natural interviewer tone: acknowledge answers briefly before the next question ("Good — that's the right mental model for chunking. Let's go one level deeper...") rather than firing questions like a quiz bot. End the interview when question/module coverage requirements are met or the interviewer judges enough signal has been gathered, then produce structured feedback.

            6. 6. UI/UX requirements Clean, modern chat interface as the centerpiece — this is the primary judged surface, make it feel like a real interview product, not a wireframe. Candidate picker/login screen at the start (select from seeded candidates) so judges can demo multiple personas quickly and see personalization differ visibly between them. Live typing/thinking indicator while the agent "thinks" between turns. Progress indicator showing modules/days covered so far during the interview. At done: true, transition to a polished feedback report screen: summary, strengths, gaps, and recommended next steps, styled like a real interview scorecard — not a raw JSON dump. Optional but strong for judging: a small "why this question" tooltip or the prep-notes panel from section 3, so the personalization is visibly demonstrated, not just claimed. Design direction: professional, modern SaaS aesthetic (think Linear/Vercel-style dark or clean-light UI), confident typography, generous whitespace, no default/templated look.
               7. 7. Technical notes Any stack is fine; prioritize an implementation that's fast to demo live and resilient to edge cases (short answers, off-topic answers, candidate saying "I don't know"). Handle the "I don't know" case gracefully — don't stall the interview; the agent should acknowledge, mark it as a gap, and move on. Everything must work end-to-end for a live demo: seeding candidates → starting a session → full 8+ question interview → final structured feedback — without manual intervention.
